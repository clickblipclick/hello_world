THE WORLD, IN WOOL
==================

A pattern for one small world, knitted in the round, seamless, as the
original is. It comes out about the size of an orange. It does not turn.
It can be thrown, which the other one cannot, and it is warm.


YOU WILL NEED

  A   the Tilde Sea    ~   a blue, or whatever the sea is where you are.
                           About thirty metres.
  B   the Hash         #   a green. Ten metres. There is less land than
                           people expect: 483 stitches of it in the chart,
                           against 1,245 of water.
  C   the Asterisks    *   white, a few metres, for the two ends.

  Double-knitting weight, or anything, so long as all three agree.
  Five double-pointed needles a size smaller than the yarn asks for, so
  that the stuffing stays in and the night stays out.
  Stuffing. A blunt needle. Twelve minutes of patience near the start.

  Tension is not critical. It is a world; nobody will try it on.


ABBREVIATIONS

  k        knit
  m1       make one: lift the strand between the stitches and knit into
           the back of it. The world gets bigger. This is the only place
           where that happens.
  k2tog    knit two together. The world gets smaller. Two places become
           one place and neither is consulted.
  rnd      a round. Elsewhere this is called a day, but these take longer.


THE SOUTH

It is begun at the bottom, like most things that are meant to stand up.

  With C, cast on 12 stitches. Share them over four needles, three each,
  and join, taking the usual care not to twist. This is the worst of it.

  Rnd 1    k.                                           12 stitches
  Rnd 2    (k1, m1) twelve times.                       24
  Rnd 3    k.
  Rnd 4    (k2, m1) twelve times.                       36
  Rnd 5    k.

  Break C. That was the ice. Join A.

  Rnd 6    (k3, m1) twelve times.                       48
  Rnd 7    k.
  Rnd 8    (k4, m1) twelve times.                       60
  Rnd 9    k.
  Rnd 10   (k5, m1) twelve times.                       72
  Rnds 11 and 12   k.

There is some land in these rounds, on the real one. It has been left
off. Shaping and geography at once is how worlds end up unfinished, and
the far south has never yet been looked at from underneath by anyone.


THE MIDDLE

Twenty-four rounds from the chart, seventy-two stitches to the round,
which is what the map has; it is the same land, taken at the moment
longitude starts. Join B.

The chart is read as all such charts are: from the bottom, and from
right to left, every round. Knitting goes westward. The world itself
goes the other way, and this has never been resolved.

Carry the colour you are not using loosely across the back. The sea is
wide, and some crossings run past thirty stitches with no land in them;
catch the idle yarn under the working one every fifth stitch or so, or
there will be a loop inside the world for the stuffing to get hold of.

Break B at the end of round 36. Do not weave the ends in. They are
going to be on the inside, with the dark.


THE NORTH

  Rnds 37 and 38   k, in A.
  Rnd 39   (k4, k2tog) twelve times.                    60
  Rnd 40   k.
  Rnd 41   (k3, k2tog) twelve times.                    48
  Rnd 42   k.
  Rnd 43   (k2, k2tog) twelve times.                    36

  Stop. Stuff it now, through the top, firmly, a little at a time,
  turning it as you go. This is the only turning it will get, so be
  thorough. It should be round. If it is a pear, take some out.

  Break A. Join C.

  Rnd 44   k.
  Rnd 45   (k1, k2tog) twelve times.                    24
  Rnd 46   k.
  Rnd 47   k2tog twelve times.                          12
  Rnd 48   k.

  Add the last of the stuffing with the blunt end of something. Break
  the yarn, thread it through the twelve stitches that are left, draw
  them up, and fasten off down through the pole and out the other side.
  That thread is the axis. It is the first one the world has had.


FINISHING

  The Caret. One stitch, in anything dark, worked as a small upward V
  over any stitch of sea. There is only one. If you make two, one of
  them is a mistake, and you will not be able to say which.

  The At. A bobble in a pale yellow, on four centimetres of chain,
  fastened anywhere along the equator. It will hang down instead of
  going round. It has one side and shows it to the floor.

  The Exclamations. Pins. Up to nine. Not for children under three,
  who will plant them elsewhere.

  The Dittos, the Shade, and the night. These are not knitted. Take it
  to a window. For the night, turn the lamp off; it will then be night
  on all of it at once, which the other one has never managed.


CARE

  Hand wash, cool. Dry flat, although it is not. Do not wring; the seas
  have nowhere to go.


Made up, it will sit where it is put and face the same way until
somebody moves it. Compare the original, which has not sat still since
it was started, and cannot be picked up.
