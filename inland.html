Inland

From the sea the Hash is one character, and is charted as dry land, and that has been thought enough. Nobody had gone in. It turns out to be mostly more Hash, with a way through it.



You are a lowercase i, as visitors are, and you have been put down at the west
shore, where there is a gap. The other gap is on the east. Between them is the interior.



  
  
  
  





Advice to walkers


The arrows will walk you, or the four buttons, or a finger drawn across the land in the direction meant.
One press is one step. You leave a mark where you have been, which is the only improvement the interior has
ever had, and it is taken up again when you leave.


There is exactly one way from any place in it to any other. This sounds like a reassurance and is offered
as a plain fact. It means there are no circles, so you cannot walk round anything, and it means that every
wrong turning ends, eventually, at a wall, which will not explain itself.


The old rule for such places is to keep one hand on the wall and not take it off. It works here. It is slow,
and visitors of one character are reminded that they have no hands.



On the survey


The interior has not been mapped and will not be. A surveyor was sent in, came out on the east side, and
handed over a careful drawing. A second was sent to check it and came out with a different careful drawing.
Both were correct. The land is laid out fresh for whoever goes in, and is not the same land twice; what you
are looking at has been seen by nobody else and will be gone when you turn round. The two drawings are kept
in separate drawers.


No record is kept of who got through, or how directly. The shortest way is known to the page and is
only mentioned afterwards, when it can do no good. Back to the world.
