A gazetteer


Nobody has been there, so the names are provisional.



  
The Hash ###

  
The bright uplands, wherever the light happens to fall. The Hash is not a place so much as a time of day;
      its inhabitants, if any, would have to keep walking east to stay in it.

  
Oland ooo

  
Middle country. Rounded hills, each the same size as every other, arranged in rows. Reputed to be pleasant.

  
The Colons :::

  
Land at dusk. Everything here is about to be explained, and then the planet turns and it isn't.

  
The Tilde Sea ~~~

  
Open water in full sun. The waves are all one wavelength, which makes navigation easy and poetry difficult.

  
The Flats ---

  
The same sea, later in the afternoon, having given up on waves.

  
Full Stop Deep ...

  
The night ocean. Sentences end here. Ships crossing it report a long pause, then land, or morning,
      whichever comes first.

  
The Asterisk Caps ***

  
Ice at both poles, each flake a footnote to something nobody wrote down. On the dark side they
      dim to plus signs, which is the only arithmetic the planet does.

  
The At @

  
The moon. It has one side and shows it to everybody. Goes around behind the world at regular
      intervals, for reasons of its own, and comes back looking exactly as it did. Not on any map,
      being the only thing here that is somewhere else.

  
The Exclamations !

  
Flags, left by visitors. The world holds nine at most; plant a tenth and the oldest quietly falls
      over. Each is kept only by the person who planted it, so no two visitors see the same claims,
      and every territorial dispute here is settled in advance by nobody else knowing about it.





  
The Apostrophes ' . `

  
The stars. Unlike the flags, they are the same for everyone, having been dealt once from a
      fixed deck. They do not turn with the world, or at all. Each one blinks now and then, to show
      that it could have been otherwise. Occasionally one of them leaves; the count is not affected.

  
The Caret ^

  
A ship, and the only one. It has no port, there being no ports, and no cargo, there being nothing
      to want. It sails until it sees land, turns away, and sails until it sees some more. The crew
      regard this as a route. It is the one thing on the world that goes anywhere the world is not
      already taking it, and it is hard to find, which is how you know it is small and not far away.

  
Elsewhere ?

  
Everything that is not the world. There is a great deal of it and none of it is listed.
      Travellers who insist on going are shown the way and, shortly afterwards, the way back.




The seas, having names, now also have a forecast.


The inhabitants, having none, nevertheless have a language, and it has a phrasebook.






Postcards


There is no post office, and nothing leaves. But the world will hold still for a picture.










Listening


It makes no sound, there being nothing for it to make a sound in. But each kind of place has been
   assigned a note, and three of them pass under the middle of the picture at any moment: one in the
   north, one on the equator, one in the south. Dragging the world plays it faster, though not better.








The calendar


A day is one turn, and takes about as long as reading this sentence slowly. A month is one trip of
   the At around the back and out again. The week has seven days, named for what the world is made of,
   and no weekend. Time began when you arrived. Dragging the world moves the date, in either direction;
   this is the only place where that works.
