A gazetteer


Nobody has been there, so the names are provisional.


who named them, then?


you did. just now, reading them.



  
The Hash ###

  
The bright uplands, wherever the light happens to fall. The Hash is not a place so much as a time of day;
      its inhabitants, if any, would have to keep walking east to stay in it.

  
Oland ooo

  
Middle country. Rounded hills, each the same size as every other, arranged in rows. Reputed to be pleasant.

  
The Colons :::

  
Land at dusk. Everything here is about to be explained, and then the planet turns and it isn't.

  
The Tilde Sea ~~~

  
Open water in full sun. The waves are all one wavelength, which makes navigation easy and poetry difficult.†

  
The Flats ---

  
The same sea, later in the afternoon, having given up on waves.

  
Full Stop Deep ...

  
The night ocean. Sentences end here. Ships crossing it report a long pause, then land, or morning,
      whichever comes first.

  
The Asterisk Caps ***

  
Ice at both poles, each flake a footnote to something nobody wrote down.* On the dark side they
      dim to plus signs, which is the only arithmetic the planet does.

  
The At @

  
The moon. It has one side and shows it to everybody. Goes around behind the world at regular
      intervals, for reasons of its own, and comes back looking exactly as it did. Not on any map,
      being the only thing here that is somewhere else.

  
The Exclamations !

  
Flags, left by visitors. The world holds nine at most; plant a tenth and the oldest quietly falls
      over. Each is kept only by the person who planted it, so no two visitors see the same claims,
      and every territorial dispute here is settled in advance by nobody else knowing about it.





  
The Apostrophes ' . `

  
The stars. Unlike the flags, they are the same for everyone, having been dealt once from a
      fixed deck. They do not turn with the world, or at all. Each one blinks now and then, to show
      that it could have been otherwise. Occasionally one of them leaves; the count is not affected.

  
The Caret ^

  
A ship, and the only one. It has no port, there being no ports, and no cargo, there being nothing
      to want. It sails until it sees land, turns away, and sails until it sees some more. The crew
      regard this as a route. It is the one thing on the world that goes anywhere the world is not
      already taking it, and it is hard to find, which is how you know it is small and not far away.

  
The Shade :.

  
An eclipse, of which there is one a month, the At being punctual. When it comes out in front it
      stands in the light, and a small round night crosses the day from west to east, taking the colour
      out of whatever it passes over. The Hash becomes the Colons for a moment and the Tilde Sea goes
      as quiet as the Deep. Nobody underneath has ever looked up, there being nobody. The Caret has been
      under it many times and each time has lit a lamp that cannot be seen from here.

  
Elsewhere ?

  
Everything that is not the world. There is a great deal of it and none of it is listed.
      Travellers who insist on going are shown the way and, shortly afterwards, the way back.

  
The Percent %

  
A bottle, when there is one. There is only ever one, and only for those who were sent it or who
      threw it in themselves. It goes where the water goes, which is east, and around anything in the way.
      The Caret has passed it several times and has never once stopped, being a ship and not a postman.

  
The Dittos " " "

  
Weather. Cloud comes over from the west in rows, each mark meaning the same as the one above it, which
      is what the mark is for. It has no opinion about coastlines and crosses the Hash and the Tilde Sea
      alike, so that for a while there is no telling which is underneath; the Caret goes in at one side and
      is not seen until it comes out at the other, and neither is a flag, though the flag is still there.
      It can only be seen by day. Whether there is cloud at night is a question for someone standing under it.

  
The Commas , ; ,

  
Small, curled, and fastened to the bottom, where they wait for the sentence to go on. They are under
      water nearly always and are uncovered only at the lowest of the ebb, for about as long as it takes
      to draw breath, which is what they are for. The only life so far reported. They have not confirmed it.

Listening


It makes no sound, there being nothing for it to make a sound in. But each kind of place has been
   assigned a note, and three of them pass under the middle of the picture at any moment: one in the
   north, one on the equator, one in the south. Dragging the world plays it faster, though not better.




There is also a recording, which is a different matter. The world was put down the wire the only
   way a picture will go: laid on its side and read out a column at a time, the high ground loud, the sea at a
   murmur, the night side hardly said at all. It lasts thirteen seconds and is not
   pleasant. It was not meant for the ear. Give it to anything that draws sound, the kind of instrument that
   puts pitch up the page and time along it, and the world comes out of the noise whole, lit from the left,
   with the two words after it in a small hand.



Those with no such instrument may listen anyway. It begins quietly, at the western edge, and is loudest a
   third of the way in, which is the Hash going by. The short chatter at the end is the greeting. It is the
   only time it has been said aloud, and it took two seconds, and nobody caught it.


For anyone who would sooner read it than hear it, which on the evidence is everyone, the recording has
   been captioned. There are eight captions and seven of them are in square
   brackets, that being how a noise is written down. They are fastened to the recording in the proper way and
   will not appear, a sound having nowhere to show them. They keep time underneath all the same, each one
   coming due and going off unseen, like most of what is said about the world while it is going round.

Questions nobody has asked


There is no enquiries desk, and so far no enquiries. The answers have been prepared anyway, to save time later.
   Each is kept shut until wanted. A question mark is Elsewhere, as the gazetteer has it; opening one turns it
   into a full stop, which is the Deep. That is roughly what being answered is like.



  How big is it?
  
About as big as it looks. This is unusual in a world and has been remarked on. Measured in its own
     characters it is a few dozen across; measured in anything else it depends how far back you are sitting.





  Why does it turn?
  
Nobody has stopped it for longer than they could hold a key down. The better question is why it turns
     east, and the answer is that east is defined here as the way it turns.





  Where does the light come from?
  
Up and to the left. There is nothing there. It has been looked for, with the telescope, and what was found
     was margin, and some stars named after it. The light arrives regardless.





  Is it safe to drag?
  
Yes. It has been dragged a great many times, in both directions, and no coastline has come loose. The Caret's
     crew feel it as weather. The Commas do not feel it at all, or have not said.





  What happens to it when I close the page?
  
Nothing that can be checked. It will be here when you come back, and will tell you how long you were gone,
     and will be right about everything except whether it missed you.





  Can I live there?
  
There is room. There is no air, fresh water, soil, or third dimension, and the ground is a different
     colour every time the sun moves. Applicants who have read this far and still wish to are advised to plant a
     flag and stand very near it.





  Who is in charge?
  
The post is vacant and has been since before the post existed. The At is the most senior object, being
     highest up, but has never given an instruction. The Caret has a captain, presumably, whose authority extends
     to the Caret, and who has used it only to turn away from land.





  Is there another one?
  
Every visitor has one, and they are all this one. They differ only in their flags, their bottle, and what
     day they think it is. Two visitors comparing worlds will agree on the stars and on nothing they did themselves.





  What comes after hello?
  
World. After that, by the look of things, quite a lot; but it was all put there afterwards, and none of
     it was promised. The greeting was complete when it was made. Everything since is the world answering.





  Why is this question here?
  
To make ten. Nobody asked for ten.





  Has anybody asked yet?
  
No. This one was put by the desk, there being no desk, to find out whether the answers open. They do.
     It is not counted with the ten, a question needing somebody at the other end of it, and it would like
     that understood before you close it again.









Pedigrees

The asterisk * now ice

  
Greek, asteriskos, a little star. Aristarchus of Samothrace, keeper of the library at Alexandria
      some twenty-two centuries ago, put it in the margin of Homer against lines that turned up again
      elsewhere in the poem. It was a mark for repetition before it was a mark for footnotes. The Caps,
      which are the same flake several hundred times, have gone back to the older trade.

  
The tilde ~ now open water

  
Spanish, out of the Latin titulus, a thing written above. Scribes who were tired of writing two
      letter n's in a row wrote one and laid a small flat second one on top of it, which is how annus
      became año. So the wave began as a letter lying down to save parchment. The sea is made entirely
      of economies.

  
The hash # now high ground

  
Generally held to be the Roman pound, libra pondo, shortened to lb with a stroke through it to show
      it was an abbreviation, then written faster and faster by clerks until only the strokes were left. The
      name octothorpe was added at a telephone laboratory in the nineteen-sixties, and those who were in the
      room have given different accounts of the thorpe. The uplands, at any rate, are sold by weight.

Sayings

The dye works

   green of the kind found on old instruments and the insides of hospitals, and the land, the sea and the ice
   were all done out of the same pot, which is why they have had to be told apart by their shapes.


There is now a vat. Choose a colour and the world goes through it whole, there being no way to dip a

The slide rule


There is one of everything here, and nobody has ever needed to know what one of a thing comes to when
   taken once. A rule for multiplying was made regardless, out of two strips of the same boxwood as the tide
   post, on the grounds that visitors arrive with numbers of their own and should not have to hold them the
   whole way down.


It is the ordinary kind. The lower scale is fixed and the upper one slides. Put the 1 of the upper over
   your first number, find your second on the upper, and the answer is underneath it, where it has been
   since the scales were cut. The rule does not work anything out. It is two lengths laid end to end, and the
   numbers were spaced so that this would do.




The rule runs on the same current as the world. Without it, it is two strips of boxwood, and still correct.

It is good to three figures on a wide screen and to two on a narrow one, and will not say which of your
   noughts it has dropped; that is for the visitor to carry. When the answer is past the right-hand end, it
   is past the right-hand end. Run the slide back the other way and read it a size smaller. The world also
   stops at its edge and has never apologised.

The bestiary


The old books of beasts were written by people who had not seen the beasts, from the reports of people who
   had not seen them either, and were trusted for centuries. This one is compiled on the same footing. No animal
   has been observed on the world. Each is drawn from the life all the same, in its box, and each, as the form
   requires, is made to stand for something.

Of the Equals

=

Two eels that swim level, one above the other, and never touch. Whatever is to the left of them is the

The sestina


A poem in an old form, chosen because the form goes round. It has six words to end its lines
   on and is allowed no others. It uses them six times in six orders and then gives them back.


Before there was a coast there was a world,
and before the world a word, and it was hello.
Somebody typed it. Nobody had thought of sea.
It sat in the dark of a page with its one light
on, and nothing behind it, not so much as a back,
and waited, as a first word will, for its turn.


That came. Things were added: to begin with, a turn,
then enough of a round to be called a world,
then, since a round thing has to have one, a back,
where the moon goes to be out of reach of hello.
From the upper left somebody let in the light,
and tildes took what room was left, and were the sea.


Nothing much is asked of a tilde sea.
It keeps to one wavelength and it waits its turn
to go from dash to dot as it loses the light.
A caret crosses it, the one ship in the world,
with no port, a cargo of perhaps one hello,
and no orders that say a thing about coming back.


You came by the front. They all do. The back
is got to by waiting, or by dragging the sea
sideways with a finger, which is a kind of hello
and is permitted. It will take up its turn
when you let go. It has been a patient world
with everyone who held it up to the light.


Once an hour something is added, by a light
hand that does not sign and does not look back
at what the last hand did. So grows the world:
a gauge, a bell, a flag, another name for sea,
each set down where it fell, to wait its turn
under the two words. Under all of it, hello.


It is not much to build a place on, a hello.
It has held. Go down as far as there is light
to read by, and each thing in the pile will turn
out to be saying it, after its fashion, back.
Six words have gone round six times here, like a sea
going round the only thing it has, a world.


Say hello, then. It was said to you first. Say it back.
The light comes off the sea one character
at a turn, and that is a day, and that is the world.
