The cloakroom

The world keeps nothing about you. It has nowhere to put it, and nobody to ask for it back. What there is of you here is kept on your own machine, on your side of the glass, and this is the counter where it can be looked at and taken away again. Nothing below was sent anywhere to be shown to you. It was read off the shelf behind you.

Flags

Planted by you, held for you, seen by nobody else. Until now one could only be got rid of by planting nine more on top of it.

    Arrivals

    How often you have come and when you last left, so that the world can say what you missed.

      The ribbon

      Where you stopped reading, if you laid the ribbon in: the name of the part, and how far through it.

        The drawer

        The keeper’s copies, for when the line is down. They are of the world and not of you, but it is your shelf they are taking up.

          Not held

          Your name, though the desk asked for it; the desk forgets it when the permit is stamped. Your bottle, which travels in its own address and is no one’s once thrown. What you sent by the arms. Your answers to the paper. Where you pointed. How long you looked. No count is kept of visitors. The census counts everything on the world, and you are not on it.

          Taking everything leaves you a first sighting again, with an unclaimed world, which is how everybody started. The world will not notice, and will be exactly as pleased to see you.

          Back to the world, where it will all begin to accumulate again at once.
