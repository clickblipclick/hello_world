The shanty

A working song, for a ship on which there is no work.

The Caret has a crew, and a crew has a song, and this is it. There is only the one. It is sung to keep time while hauling, and since there is nothing aboard to haul it is sung to keep time while turning away from land, which is the only thing the ship does that takes more than one person to agree on.

The plain lines belong to the shantyman, a post that has never been filled. The lines set in from the margin belong to everybody else, and those are sung with some force.

  1. We left from no­where, as you do, A­way, a­way, and round we go! And no­where's where we're head­ing to. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  2. We have no port, there be­ing none, A­way, a­way, and round we go! We car­ry no­thing, by the ton. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  3. We steer by not quite hit­ting things, A­way, a­way, and round we go! It's all the chart the morn­ing brings. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  4. The At goes round the back a­lone, A­way, a­way, and round we go! And what it does there is not known. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  5. We passed a bot­tle, go­ing east, A­way, a­way, and round we go! It did not need us in the least. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  6. Some gi­ant plant­ed flags a­shore, A­way, a­way, and round we go! We count­ed nine and ne­ver more. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  7. The cloud came o­ver from the west, A­way, a­way, and round we go! We put our col­lars up and guessed. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  8. There's some­one watch­ing from the sky, A­way, a­way, and round we go! We do not wave as we go by. There's land a­head, so turn her round!
  9. And when the page is closed at night, A­way, a­way, and round we go! We car­ry on with­out the light. There's land a­head, so turn her round!

There is no tenth verse. After the ninth the song is started again from the first, the route being what it is. Verses have been proposed about arriving, and were voted down as speculative.

It is in the Dorian mode, which is the minor key of people who are not actually unhappy. Six quavers to the bar, two bars to the line, and the last note is the only one that sounds like stopping. The crew dislike it for that reason and tend to come in early with the next verse.

The ship this is sung aboard is small and hard to find. It is on the world.
