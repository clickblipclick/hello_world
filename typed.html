Before the two words are anything else, somebody types them. This page is about the hands: which keys, which fingers, how many presses. At the end there is a place to type them yourself. This list only grows too. Back to the first page.

  • Seven keys and the space barThe layout is from 1873 and was made for a typewriter. Of its twenty-six letter keys, nineteen are never touched.
  • 5 + 5 + 0
    Top row, middle row, bottom rowe, o, w, o, r are reached for; h, l, l, l, d are on the row where the fingers already rest. Nothing is on the bottom row.
  • 4 + 6
    Left hand, right handThe left takes e, w, r, d and the right takes h, l, l, o, o, l. The hands change over seven times. Either thumb may have the space.
  • l l o o l
    One finger does half the workThe right ring finger types both the l and the o above it, so it presses five of the ten letters. Neither little finger is needed at all.
  • 44 33 555 555 666 0 9 666 777 555 3
    With one thumb, on twelve keysHow it was typed on a telephone before telephones had keyboards: press a key until its letter comes round. Twenty-five presses for eleven characters, and a pause between the two l's. The plain numbers are on the page of sounds.
  • At forty words a minute, an ordinary speed, it takes 3.3 seconds. The page will time you from the first key to the last.
