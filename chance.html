On every other page somebody meant it. Here nobody does: the keys are pressed at random, and the question is how long until the two words turn up anyway. The keyboard has twenty-seven keys, the letters and a space bar. This list only grows too. Back to the first page.

  • 1 in 27
    to get the hThe first key is the easy one. About four presses in every hundred are right.
  • 1 in 14,348,907
    to get as far as helloTwenty-seven, five times over. At ten keys a second that is a little over sixteen days of typing, on average, for the first word alone.
  • 1 in 5,559,060,566,555,523
    to get all elevenFive and a half quadrillion. The space in the middle has to be hit by chance as well, and it costs as much as any letter.
  • 17,600,000 years
    of typing, at ten keys a secondThat is the average wait, with no breaks. The typist would have had to start before there were any people to greet.
  • 1913
    when the monkeys were hiredÉmile Borel, a French mathematician, imagined a million of them at typewriters for ten hours a day, to show how unlikely the unlikely is. They have been typing in arguments ever since.

    The same typist, allowed to keep what worksStart from eleven random keys. Make a hundred copies, each with a few mistakes, keep whichever copy is nearest, and repeat. Richard Dawkins did this with a line of Hamlet in 1986. Chance alone needs millions of years; chance with a memory needs a few seconds.
