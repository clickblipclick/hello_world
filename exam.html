The Provisional Board of Examiners

Geography

Paper One: the World

Two hours, or about eight hundred days, whichever the candidate finds longer

  • Answer all questions. Where a question has no answer, answer it anyway; this is how the gazetteer was written.
  • Candidates are reminded that nobody has been there. Fieldwork will not be credited.
  • Write on one side of the paper only. The At manages with one side and so can you.
  • The world may be consulted during the examination. It will not help.

Section A: short answers

  1. [1]How many ships are there?
  2. [2]A visitor plants ten flags. State how many remain, and say what became of the other.
  3. [2]Distinguish between the Tilde Sea and the Flats. Candidates who answer “about three hours” will receive both marks and need write nothing further.
  4. [3]The bottle goes east. The cloud comes from the west. The Shade crosses from west to east. Name one thing on the world that goes west, and explain why you were unable to.
  5. [1]Which of the following is at Elsewhere?

Section C: calculation

  1. [3]There are seventy stars. One of them leaves. How many stars are there? Give your answer to the nearest seventy.
  2. [4]The Caret sets out from no port carrying no cargo and arrives nowhere. Calculate the profit on the voyage. Then calculate it again as the crew would, who regard this as a route.
  3. [4]The week has seven days and no weekend. A candidate began this paper on the first of them. On which day will the candidate be allowed to stop? Express your answer as a sigh.
  4. [2]The Asterisk Caps dim to plus signs on the dark side. Using only this, the planet’s whole arithmetic, add the north pole to the south pole.

Section D: essay

  1. [20]Answer one of the following. Credit will be given for length of pause.
    1. “The Hash is not a place so much as a time of day.” Discuss, walking east as you do so.
    2. Every territorial dispute on the world is settled in advance by nobody else knowing about it. Assess this as a system of government, and say whether you would like it at home.
    3. The sea is made of one wavelength, which makes navigation easy and poetry difficult. Write the poem. You have one wavelength.
    4. Is the world stopped, or is it waiting for you to leave? Do not ask the robots; they were told what to say.
