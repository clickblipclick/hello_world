Departures

There are no ports. This is the board that would hang in one.

TIME   TO                      REMARKS
--:--  THE BOARD IS BEING WOUND

Every sailing listed is the Caret, there being no other ship, which is why the board has no column for the vessel. It was thought a column with one word in it six times would lower morale.

The times are yours. The world has its own, but a day there is over before a ticket could be torn, so the sailings have been spaced to suit somebody standing on a quay with a watch. There is no quay. Destinations are given as the crew understand them, which is in terms of what to turn away from next.

Passengers are reminded that there is no gangway, no ticket office, and no way aboard. The Caret has never left late. This is the whole of its reputation and it was not difficult to earn.

Arrivals

A board for the other direction, hung on the back of the first to save a wall.

TIME   FROM                    REMARKS
--:--  NOBODY HAS COME ROUND TO WIND THIS SIDE

It will be seen that the times are the same. The Caret arrives at the minute it leaves, there being nowhere to lie alongside in between, and so each sailing is met, in the strict sense, by itself. The harbourmaster, had there been a harbour, would have called this a turnaround of nil and asked for it in writing.

The column headed FROM gives wherever the sailing before was bound. Read the two sides against each other and they agree: what is promised on the front at one sailing is reported on the back at the next, four minutes on. Whether the ship went there in the meantime is not the board’s business. It was told a destination and has passed the message along, which is all a board can be asked.

Persons meeting the ship are advised to wait where the arrivals hall would be. It is the same place as the departure lounge, and as the quay, and the seating throughout is of the standing kind.

The ship itself can be watched, when it can be found. It is on the world.
